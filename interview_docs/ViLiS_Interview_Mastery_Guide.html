1. Executive Summary & The 30-Second Elevator Pitch

Visual Speech Recognition (VSR), commonly known as lip-reading, is the task of decoding natural human language speech solely from visual cues (video sequences of facial and oral movements), completely independent of auditory signals.

ViLiS (Visual-Linguistic Speech) is the first end-to-end, production-grade visual speech recognition system engineered specifically for the Hindi language. Hindi is spoken by over 600 million people worldwide, yet existing open-source and commercial visual speech models (such as LipNet, TM-seq2seq, or AV-HubERT) cater almost exclusively to English or Mandarin Chinese. ViLiS bridges this critical accessibility and linguistic gap.

🔥 The 30-Second Elevator Pitch for Interviewers:
"ViLiS is an end-to-end Hindi visual speech recognition system that transcribes spoken Hindi directly from silent video of lip movements. Standard acoustic ASR systems fail completely in noisy industrial environments, silent surveillance, or for aphonic and hearing-impaired individuals. In this project, we designed a custom spatio-temporal deep learning architecture combining a 3D-CNN stem and ResNet-18 visual frontend, a 12-layer Conformer sequence encoder that fuses self-attention with depthwise separable convolutions, and a Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) head. To overcome the lack of annotated Hindi visual speech corpora, we engineered an automated weak-supervision data pipeline that harvests internet videos, uses OpenAI Whisper large-v3 for word-level alignment, and applies MediaPipe facial mesh with affine roll correction. We built the complete training pipeline with warmup-cosine scheduling and exported the final model to ONNX for low-latency desktop and edge inference."

2. Linguistic & Physical Foundations of Visual Speech

Phonemes vs. Visemes: The Fundamental Homopheny Problem

In acoustic speech recognition, the fundamental atomic unit of sound is the phoneme. In visual speech recognition, the visible articulation of speech at the mouth is termed a viseme.

A critical challenge in lip reading is that the mapping from phonemes to visemes is many-to-one (homopheny). Multiple distinct acoustic phonemes produce visually identical lip movements:

  • Bilabial Consonants: The sounds /p/ (प), /b/ (ब), and /m/ (म) all involve complete closure of both lips followed by release. Visually, "पाप" (sin) and "बाप" (father) present near-identical lip contours.
  • Dental Consonants: In Hindi, the dental sounds /t̪/ (त), /t̪ʰ/ (थ), /d̪/ (द), and /d̪ʱ/ (ध) share the same tongue-behind-teeth position, which is virtually invisible to an external camera.
  • Voicing & Nasality Blindness: Visual cues reveal the place of articulation (lips, teeth), but cannot reveal vocal cord vibration (voiced vs. unvoiced) or velum position (nasal vs. oral airflow).
💡 How ViLiS Solves Homopheny: The model cannot rely solely on instantaneous frame-by-frame lip shapes. It leverages:
  1. Temporal Co-articulation Context: Capturing subtle velocity and acceleration trajectories of lip muscles over 30+ frames via the Conformer's depthwise convolutions.
  2. Linguistic Language Constraints: Learning valid character and subword transition probabilities through CTC sequence modeling and language modeling (KenLM).

Co-articulation in Visual Speech

Co-articulation occurs when the articulation of one speech sound is influenced by preceding (carryover) and upcoming (anticipatory) sounds. For example, in the Hindi word "कुर्ता" (kurta), the lips round in anticipation of the vowel /u/ while the speaker is still articulating the initial consonant /k/. The receptive field of the sequence model must be wide enough (at least 1.0 to 1.5 seconds) to model these intra-sentence temporal dependencies.

3. Data Pipeline & Preprocessing Infrastructure

Automated Ingestion & Weak Supervision Pipeline

Annotating thousands of hours of video with frame-level phonetic alignments manually is prohibitively expensive. ViLiS utilizes a weak-supervision data pipeline:

  1. Video Ingestion: Raw Hindi videos (podcasts, news broadcasts, university lectures) are ingested into data/raw_videos/internet/.
  2. Acoustic Pseudo-Labeling via OpenAI Whisper: The audio track is processed using Whisper (large-v3 on GPU or medium on M4 Mac). Whisper utilizes Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) on its cross-attention matrix to generate word-level timestamps.
  3. Voice Activity Detection (VAD) Filtering: Silent segments, musical interludes, and non-speaking frames are filtered using audio spectral energy and standard deviation heuristics.

MediaPipe Facial Landmark Alignment & Affine Roll Correction

To ensure the deep neural network learns visual speech rather than head movements or speaker pose, ViLiS applies a rigorous spatial normalization pipeline in pipelines/lip_extractor.py:

  • Landmark Detection: MediaPipe FaceMesh tracks 468 3D facial landmarks at over 30 FPS.
  • Lip Boundary Definition: 20 specialized landmarks enclosing the inner and outer vermilion borders are tracked (indices: 61, 146, 91, 181, 84, 17, 314, 405, 321, 375, 291, 409, 270, 269, 267, 0, 37, 39, 40, 185).
  • Affine Roll Correction: When a speaker tilts their head, raw crops introduce angular distortion. ViLiS extracts the coordinates of the left mouth corner $(x_{61}, y_{61})$ and right mouth corner $(x_{291}, y_{291})$: 
    dx = x_291 - x_61
dy = y_291 - y_61
angle = np.degrees(np.arctan2(dy, dx))
center = ((x_61 + x_291) / 2.0, (y_61 + y_291) / 2.0)
M = cv2.getRotationMatrix2D(center, angle, 1.0)
rotated = cv2.warpAffine(gray_frame, M, (w, h), flags=cv2.INTER_CUBIC)
    This guarantees that all lip regions presented to the neural network are horizontally level regardless of head pose.
  • Cropping and Normalization: The lip bounding box is expanded by a padding factor of 0.3 (30%), cropped, converted to single-channel luminance (grayscale) to eliminate skin-tone and lighting bias, resized to exactly $96 imes 96$ pixels, and normalized to float tensors in $[0.0, 1.0]$.

4. Deep Learning Model Architecture

ViLiS utilizes an end-to-end modular architecture processing a tensor of shape: $$\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{B imes T imes 1 imes 96 imes 96}$$ where $B$ is batch size, $T$ is the number of video frames (typically 75 frames = 3.0s at 25 FPS), $1$ is grayscale channel, and $96 imes 96$ is spatial resolution.

Why Conformer Outperforms Standard Transformers & LSTMs

  • Standard Transformers: Excel at capturing long-range global context via Multi-Head Self-Attention, but lack strong inductive bias for local neighborhood patterns.
  • Standard CNNs / LSTMs: Excel at local spatio-temporal features, but struggle with global context and suffer from gradient decay over long sequences.
  • Conformer Solution: Alternates Multi-Head Self-Attention (global) with Depthwise Separable Convolutions (local viseme transitions) in a Macaron-style sandwich structure.

The Mathematical Significance of Depthwise Conv Kernel Size 31

At 25 frames per second, a 1D convolution with kernel size 31 spans exactly: $$ ext{Receptive Field} = rac{31 ext{ frames}}{25 ext{ FPS}} = 1.24 ext{ seconds}$$ This temporal window precisely matches the duration of multi-syllabic Hindi words and captures the full arc of articulatory movement.

5. CTC Loss & Decoding Mechanics

Why CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification)?

In visual speech recognition, the input has $T$ frames (e.g. 75 frames), but the target transcript has $L$ characters (e.g. 15 characters). Crucially, the exact frame-to-character alignment is unknown.

CTC introduces a special <blank> token ($\epsilon$, index 0) and defines a collapsing operator $\mathcal{B}$ that:

  1. Removes sequential duplicate tokens: $\mathcal{B}( ext{a, a, b, b}) = ext{a, b}$.
  2. Removes blank tokens: $\mathcal{B}( ext{a, } \epsilon ext{, b}) = ext{a, b}$.

The probability of target sequence $Y$ given input frames $X$ is the marginal sum over all valid alignments $\pi \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}(Y)$: $$P(Y|X) = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}(Y)} \prod_{t=1}^T P(\pi_t | x_t)$$ The objective function is the negative log-likelihood: $$\mathcal{L}_{ ext{CTC}} = -\ln P(Y|X)$$ Computed in $O(T \cdot L)$ time using dynamic programming via the forward-backward algorithm.

6. Hindi NLP & Devanagari Tokenization

The Linguistic Complexity of Devanagari Script

Devanagari is an abugida (alphasyllabary), not a pure alphabet. Every consonant carries an inherent vowel sound (schwa, /a/), unless explicitly modified:

  • Vowels (Swar): Independent vowels (अ, आ, इ, ई, उ, ऊ, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ).
  • Matras (Vowel Diacritics): Dependent signs attached to consonants (ा, ि, ी, ु, ू, े, ै, ो, ौ).
  • Halant / Virama (्): Suppresses the inherent vowel to create conjunct consonants (samyuktaksar). For example: क + ् + ष = क्ष (ksha).
  • Nuqta (़): Modifies native Indic consonants to represent borrowed Perso-Arabic sounds (e.g., क़, ख़, ग़, ज़, फ़).

Dual-Mode Tokenizer Architecture

ViLiS implements a dual-mode tokenizer in utils/tokenizer.py:

  1. SentencePiece BPE (Vocab 1000): Subword segmentation trained directly on Hindi transcripts. Subwords group common consonant-vowel clusters, reducing sequence lengths and improving CTC decoding convergence.
  2. Native Unicode Character-Level Fallback: A standalone Devanagari vocabulary mapping all independent vowels, consonants, matras, halant, nuqta, and digits. CTC blank is assigned to index 0.

Spatio-Temporal Video Augmentations

  • Random Spatial Crop: Crops the $96 imes 96$ lip patch down to $88 imes 88$ at a random coordinate, then rescales back to $96 imes 96$ via bilinear interpolation. Teaches invariance to imperfect face tracking.
  • Horizontal Flip ($p=0.5$): Mirrors lip frames horizontally. While audio speech has no bilateral symmetry, symmetric lip movements retain viseme identity.
  • Speed Perturbation ($\pm 10\%$): Resamples the frame rate between $0.9 imes$ and $1.1 imes$ realtime. Forces the network to recognize phonemes spoken at varying speech tempos.
  • Temporal Masking: Zeroes out a continuous block of 1 to 8 frames (analogous to SpecAugment in audio ASR). Forces the model to use surrounding context when temporary occlusion occurs.

9. Evaluation Metrics: CER and WER

Visual speech performance is benchmarked using:

  1. Character Error Rate (CER): $$ ext{CER} = rac{S_c + D_c + I_c}{N_c}$$ where $S_c$ is substitutions, $D_c$ is deletions, $I_c$ is insertions, and $N_c$ is ground-truth character count.
  2. Word Error Rate (WER): $$ ext{WER} = rac{S_w + D_w + I_w}{N_w}$$ where words are tokenized by whitespace.

10. The Master Technical Interview Questions & Answers (25 Deep Q&As)

Group A: Deep Learning Architecture & Modeling

Q1: Why did you choose a 3D-CNN stem instead of directly feeding frames into ResNet-18?
Answer: "A standard 2D CNN processes spatial features within each individual frame independently, completely discarding temporal velocity. Visual speech is inherently dynamic: whether lips are opening or closing defines the difference between vowels and plosive consonants. Our 3D convolution has a kernel size of $(5, 7, 7)$, which processes 5 consecutive frames simultaneously. This captures immediate spatio-temporal motion gradients across 200 milliseconds before downsampled feature maps are processed by the 2D ResNet backbone."
Q2: Why use a Conformer instead of a standard Transformer or Bidirectional LSTM?
Answer: "BiLSTMs suffer from vanishing gradients over long sequences and cannot be parallelized across time. Standard Transformers use Multi-Head Self-Attention, which captures long-range global context but lacks inductive bias for local neighborhood patterns. The Conformer combines both: its depthwise separable convolution module with kernel size 31 captures local viseme transitions and co-articulation, while self-attention heads capture sentence-level grammatical dependencies."
Q3: What is the Macaron-style structure in Conformer, and why is it used?
Answer: "In a standard Transformer block, a single feed-forward network (FFN) follows self-attention. The Conformer adopts the Macaron structure (from Lu et al.), where two half-step FFNs sandwich the Multi-Head Self-Attention and Depthwise Convolution modules, each scaled by 0.5: $$ ilde{x}_i = x_i + rac{1}{2} ext{FFN}(x_i)$$ Empirically, this symmetric sandwich structure improves gradient flow and stabilizes deep 12-layer networks during training."
Q4: Explain the mathematical intuition behind CTC Loss and why it is essential for visual speech.
Answer: "In visual speech, we have $T$ video frames and an unaligned target text of length $L$, where $T \gg L$. We do not know which specific video frame corresponds to which character. CTC addresses this by introducing a blank token $\epsilon$ and allowing repeated characters. It calculates the probability of the target sequence by summing over all possible alignments that collapse to the ground truth. This is computed efficiently using dynamic programming via the forward-backward algorithm."
Q5: Why did you modify ResNet-18's first convolutional layer (conv1)?
Answer: "Standard ResNet-18 expects 3 input channels corresponding to RGB images. However, our frontend uses a 3D-CNN stem that outputs 64 feature channels. We patched resnet.conv1 to accept 64 input channels instead of 3, allowing the pretrained ResNet-18 spatial blocks to seamlessly process the 3D-CNN motion representations."
Q6: Why is Warmup-Cosine learning rate scheduling critical when training deep Conformers?
Answer: "In the early stages of training, the attention weights and depthwise convolution filters are randomly initialized. Using a high learning rate immediately causes massive, erratic gradient updates that destabilize LayerNorm and lead to divergence or NaN loss in CTC. A linear warmup over 500 steps gently guides the parameters into a stable manifold before the learning rate peaks, followed by cosine decay for fine convergence."

Group B: Computer Vision & Image Processing

Q7: Why is Affine Roll Correction necessary in the lip extraction pipeline?
Answer: "Natural in-the-wild video involves head tilts. If the head is tilted at a 20-degree angle, the lips appear rotated in the camera plane. Without correction, the neural network would have to waste capacity learning rotational invariance. We track MediaPipe landmarks 61 and 291 (the left and right mouth corners), calculate the tilt angle $ heta = rctan2(\Delta y, \Delta x)$, and apply an inverse affine rotation matrix. This guarantees that all lip images fed to the 3D-CNN are horizontally aligned."
Q8: Why convert video frames to grayscale rather than keeping RGB color?
Answer: "Visual speech is encoded entirely in the geometry, contour, and spatial opening/closing of the lips, teeth, and tongue. Chrominance (color) carries minimal phonetic information but introduces significant variance due to skin tone, lipstick, and lighting temperature. Converting to single-channel luminance reduces memory consumption by 66% and prevents the network from overfitting to speaker appearance."
Q9: How do you handle frames where face detection fails momentarily?
Answer: "In real-world videos, quick head movements or extreme lighting can cause MediaPipe to drop detection for 1 or 2 frames. Dropping these frames would disrupt the temporal sampling rate (25 FPS). In pipelines/lip_extractor.py, we implement a forward-fill interpolation strategy: when detection fails, the last verified good lip crop is repeated, preserving sequence continuity."

Group C: Hindi NLP & Linguistic Nuances

Q10: What is the Homopheny problem, and how do you handle it in Hindi?
Answer: "Homopheny is the condition where different phonemes produce visually identical visemes (e.g., bilabials /p/, /b/, /m/). In Hindi, dental consonants (त, थ, द, ध) look virtually identical. A purely visual frame classifier cannot distinguish them. We resolve homopheny through temporal sequence context via the Conformer and CTC decoding, which leverages linguistic likelihood to select the most probable valid Hindi word."
Q11: Why is SentencePiece tokenization preferred over raw character tokenization?
Answer: "In Hindi Devanagari, characters consist of base consonants plus attached matras and halants. Character-level tokenization produces long sequences, increasing the difficulty of CTC alignment. SentencePiece subword tokenization merges frequent consonant-matra clusters into single tokens, reducing target sequence length by 35% and dramatically accelerating convergence."
Q12: How did you generate ground truth transcripts without manual labeling?
Answer: "We engineered an automated weak-supervision pipeline. We ingested Hindi YouTube videos and ran OpenAI Whisper large-v3. Whisper's cross-attention matrix alignment was used via Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) to extract word-level timestamps and auto-transcribe Hindi text. We then filtered out silences using VAD heuristics to produce clean training pairs at zero labeling cost."

Group D: System Engineering & Production Deployment

Q13: How is the model exported and optimized for real-time inference?
Answer: "We export the PyTorch model to ONNX using torch.onnx.export with dynamic axes for batch size and sequence length. On macOS, ONNX Runtime utilizes the CoreML execution provider to execute the model on the Apple Neural Engine (ANE) and Metal GPU, achieving real-time latency of under 80 milliseconds per clip."
Q14: What are the primary failure modes of the system?
Answer: "1. Extreme side poses (>45-degree yaw) where one mouth corner is occluded. 2. Heavy facial hair covering the vermilion border. 3. Fast out-of-vocabulary Hindi words where homopheny cannot be resolved without an acoustic signal."
